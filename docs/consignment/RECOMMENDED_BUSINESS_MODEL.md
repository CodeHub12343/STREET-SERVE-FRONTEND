# Recommended Business Model

**This document is a recommendation, not a description of the current system.**

---

## The recommendation in one sentence

> Build a **hybrid, digital-first consignment marketplace** where the customer's payment flows
> **into the platform** and is split three ways using Stripe *separate charges and transfers*, while
> **cash sales are recorded as a bounded debt** owed by the seller — with a **double-entry ledger**
> underneath everything.

Three components, in strict priority order:

1. **The ledger** — double-entry accounting for every movement. *Build this first.*
2. **The digital rail** — in-app customer payment, split three ways.
3. **The cash rail** — cash sales become seller debt, capped by trust tier, recovered by netting.

---

## Why the ledger comes first

It is tempting to build payments first because that is the visible gap. That would be a mistake.

Every other fix — refunds, disputes, debt, failed-transfer retries, partial settlement, tax
reporting — requires the ability to answer *"what does each party currently owe or hold?"* Today
nothing can answer that. Retrofitting a ledger underneath live money movements is dramatically harder
than building the ledger first and routing money through it.

**Principle: money never moves without a balanced ledger entry.**

```
Every event writes balanced double-entry rows:

  Customer pays $903 (digital)
    DR  Platform cash                 $903.00
    CR  Payable to seller             $528.25
    CR  Payable to hub                $284.45
    CR  Platform fee revenue           $90.30
                                    ─────────
                          balanced:   $903.00

  Payouts execute
    DR  Payable to seller             $528.25      CR Platform cash  $528.25
    DR  Payable to hub                $284.45      CR Platform cash  $284.45

  Cash sale of $903 instead
    DR  Receivable from seller        $374.75      ← the seller OWES this
    CR  Payable to hub                $284.45
    CR  Platform fee revenue           $90.30
    (no platform cash moves — correctly)
```

The cash case is the proof of why this works: the same sale produces a *receivable* instead of
*cash*, and the hub is still owed exactly what it is owed. The platform's books stay true in both
rails, which is impossible without double entry.

## The digital rail

### The critical Stripe decision: separate charges and transfers

The orders module today uses **destination charges** (`application_fee_amount` +
`transfer_data.destination`). That is correct for orders, where one vendor gets paid — but it
**cannot serve consignment**, because a destination charge pays exactly **one** connected account and
consignment must split **three** ways.

| Approach | 3-way split? | Merchant of record | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Destination charge | ❌ one payee only | Platform | Cannot work for consignment |
| Direct charge on seller | ❌ | Seller | Seller isn't the merchant — the hub owns the goods |
| **Separate charges + transfers** | ✅ | Platform | **Recommended** |

```
Customer pays $903
        ▼
PaymentIntent WITHOUT transfer_data  →  funds land on PLATFORM balance
        ▼
Then, in one transfer_group = "sale_<saleId>":
        ├─ transfer $528.25 → seller's connected account
        ├─ transfer $284.45 → hub's connected account
        └─ $90.30 retained  → platform revenue
```

**The settlement code already uses `createTransfer` with a `transfer_group`.** The hard half of this
is written. What's missing is the inbound charge.

**Accept the trade-offs consciously:** being merchant of record means the platform carries chargeback
liability, must handle refunds from its own balance, and takes on marketplace-facilitator tax
obligations. These are real costs — and they are the price of a marketplace that actually controls
its money. Every platform in the comparison table pays them.

### How the customer pays

A **QR code or payment link generated by the seller's phone** — no card reader, no customer app
install. The seller picks product + quantity; the server prices it from the checkout snapshot (never
the client); the customer scans and pays with the phone already in their hand.

Hardware is capital. The target seller has none. Square's reader business is not a model to copy here.

## The cash rail

Cash cannot be banned — attempting it just produces under-reporting. So model it:

```
Seller logs a CASH sale of $903
        ▼
No platform money moves
        ▼
Ledger records:  seller owes $374.75  (hub share + platform fee)
        ▼
Recovered, in order of preference:
   1. Automatically netted from the seller's next DIGITAL payouts
   2. Settled in-app by card by the seller
   3. New checkouts blocked once debt exceeds the trust-tier limit
```

**The netting mechanism is what makes this humane and effective.** Rather than chasing a low-income
seller for money, their next digital sale simply pays less out until the balance clears. No
collections calls, no debt spiral — and a natural incentive to take digital payments.

### Bounding the risk with trust

| Tier | Max inventory value held | Cash debt ceiling | Payout timing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unverified | $0 (no checkout) | — | — |
| Bronze | $200 | $100 | 3-day hold |
| Silver | $1,000 | $400 | 1-day hold |
| Gold | $5,000 | $1,500 | Instant |
| Shelter-cosigned | Guarantor-backed limit | Per agreement | 3-day hold |

This finally gives the Trust Score **economic teeth**: it governs how much stock you may hold, how
much cash exposure you may carry, and how fast you get paid.

**Fix the inverted default:** new sellers currently start at trust **100 (maximum)**. Trust must be
earned — start new accounts low and let verified sales raise them.

### Make digital the obvious choice — with pricing, not policing

| | Digital sale | Cash sale |
|---|---|---|
| Platform fee | **8%** | 10% |
| Payout | Per tier, as fast as instant | Creates debt |
| Trust growth | Faster (verified) | Slower (self-reported) |
| Seller admin | None | Must track a balance |

The 2% differential prices the genuine risk difference. It converts behaviour through incentive
rather than enforcement — and it is honest, because cash *is* more expensive for the platform to
carry.

## What we keep unchanged

The recommendation deliberately preserves the well-built parts:

- Bailment model and versioned agreement
- Terms snapshot onto each checkout
- QR + condition-photo custody evidence *(hardened — see below)*
- The atomic oversell guard
- The approval gate with trust/value auto-approval
- The versioned fee registry (which now gains `consignment_digital` and `consignment_cash` types)
- Immutable financial records — **extended** with reversal entries rather than replaced

## Edge cases the model must survive

| Case | Required behaviour |
|---|---|
| **Partial sale** | Settle only sold units; unsold return to stock. Already handled. |
| **Refund before settlement** | Reverse the sale, restore stock, no payout occurred |
| **Refund after settlement** | `reverse_transfer` on both legs + refund the application fee; write **reversal entries** — never mutate the original |
| **Chargeback** | Freeze pending payouts for that seller; platform absorbs, recovers per policy; trust penalty |
| **Lost inventory** | Charge the seller the wholesale value as debt; cap by trust tier; trust penalty proportional to value |
| **Damaged inventory** | Hub assesses; partial liability charge; dispute path if contested |
| **Seller disappears with stock** | Overdue sweep → debt for full value → trust to zero → collections/ban; shelter guarantor invoked if applicable |
| **Split tender (part cash, part card)** | Two sale legs against one checkout; each routes to its own rail |
| **Offline sale** | Queue locally, sync on reconnect; idempotency key prevents doubles |
| **Payment succeeds, app crashes** | Webhook reconciles charge → sale; never rely on the client |
| **Seller sells below minimum price** | Already blocked unless permitted. Keep. |
| **Hub deregisters with stock outstanding** | Block deregistration until all checkouts settle |
| **Dispute open at settlement time** | **Hold settlement** until resolved (currently it does not) |
| **Seller owes debt and requests new stock** | Block checkout above their tier's debt ceiling |
| **Platform balance insufficient for refund** | Queue with alerting; never let a transfer fail silently |
| **Two customers buy the last unit simultaneously** | Atomic decrement — already correct |

## Compliance obligations this creates

Being merchant of record is not free. Plan for it:

| Obligation | Trigger | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **Marketplace facilitator sales tax** | Processing customer payments | Enable Stripe Tax; calculate, collect, remit per state |
| **1099-K / seller tax reporting** | Sellers crossing thresholds | Stripe Connect tax reporting; collect W-9 at onboarding |
| **KYC/AML** | Higher payout volumes | Stripe handles; scale requirements to value held |
| **Consumer refund rights** | Any retail sale | The refund flow above |
| **Money transmission** | Holding funds between parties | Mitigated by Stripe Connect if structured as above — get legal confirmation |
| **Record retention** | Financial records | Already strong; keep immutability |

`STRIPE_TAX_ENABLED=false` and zeroed tax rates today. This becomes a **legal requirement**, not a
feature, the moment payments run through the platform.

## Security hardening to include

- **Rotate the QR secret.** Today it is one static string; anyone who photographs it can reserve
  stock remotely. Use a short-lived signed token (rotating display) or require hub-side confirmation.
- **Server-side pricing only.** The payment amount must derive from the checkout snapshot, never
  from the client.
- **Idempotency on every money path.** Already present at settlement; extend to charges and refunds.
- **Move settlement off the request path** into a queued job with retries.

## Why this is the right answer

**Most scalable** — revenue grows with volume instead of draining with it; the ledger supports
multi-currency, multi-region and new fee types without redesign.

**Most profitable** — fees are collected automatically at the moment of payment rather than invoiced
after the fact. It also unlocks adjacent revenue the current model cannot reach: payment processing
margin, instant-payout fees, insurance on high-value stock, and financing built on real repayment
data.

**Safest** — the platform never pays out money it does not hold; cash risk is capped by earned trust;
refunds and disputes become possible; and every movement is auditable.

**Best experience** — sellers get faster, verified payouts and no bookkeeping; hubs get trustworthy
numbers instead of self-reported ones; customers get a receipt and a refund right; and the platform
finally gets the demand-side data its AI features were built to use.

## Features worth considering that aren't in scope today

- **Inventory insurance** — a small per-checkout premium covering loss/damage, removing the hub's
  biggest objection to lending stock to unproven sellers.
- **Seller cash advances** — once repayment data exists, advance working capital against it. This is
  the highest-margin product a marketplace like this can build, and it directly serves the mission.
- **Hub-to-hub transfers** — rebalance stock between hubs (the AI dashboard already suggests
  reallocation but nothing can act on it).
- **Dynamic split pricing** — better sellers earn a better split automatically.
- **Shelter guarantor programme** — finish the co-sign concept the UI already hints at; it is the
  answer to "how does a seller with no history get their first stock?"
- **Customer accounts and loyalty** — the demand side that makes the marketplace defensible.

---

## The honest summary

The current consignment module is a **well-built logistics system with no business model attached**.
The physical layer — custody, terms, trust, approvals — is genuinely good and should be kept. What
it needs is the financial layer brought up to the same standard: money that actually enters the
system, a ledger that keeps track of it, and an honest treatment of the cash that street commerce
will always involve.
