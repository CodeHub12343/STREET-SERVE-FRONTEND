Distinct console identity

  • Green brand ring/logo (vs. the Vendor console's orange) — a business that is both a vendor and a hub switches between two visually distinct consoles, never confusing which hat they're wearing. Same DashboardShell skeleton, different accent.
  • Hub nav is organized around the hub's actual jobs (Inventory / Money / Insights), not mirrored from the vendor's operate/manage/grow — the two roles have genuinely different daily work.

H-01 — the QR is the physical bridge

  • Registration ends by generating the printable pickup/return QR — the physical object that ties the digital `inventory_checkouts` record to a real counter (the same code sellers scan in S-06/S-09). Setup isn't complete until that bridge exists.

H-02 — terms are per-product, shown as chips

  • Split %, return window, and listing type (consignment/donation-based/wholesale/rental) surface as scannable chips per row — a hub managing dozens of products needs to compare terms at a glance, and these are exactly the fields a seller sees on S-04, kept consistent across both sides.

H-03 — the trust model made operational

  • Every pending checkout shows the seller's Trust Score, tier, and declared value — the hub is accepting real liability risk (their goods leaving the building), so the exact signals that inform that decision are front and center, per FR-8's approval flow.
  • Shelter-cosigned sellers are visibly flagged (gold chip) — a hub knows when the shelter partner is the guarantor rather than the seller's own history, so a thin Trust Score reads correctly in context rather than as a red flag.
  • Auto-approve rule for Gold-tier under a threshold is stated at the top — the manual queue only holds what genuinely needs a human decision (FR-8.4 configurable approval).

H-04 — this is the hub's version of the live map

  • Same live-map concept as the customer C-10, but inverted: the hub sits at the center marker and watches its own inventory move outward on sellers — answering "where are my goods right now" (the Live Inventory Map from the concept doc).
  • The overdue seller pin turns amber — the one piece of the map that needs the hub's attention is the one that changes color, consistent with the traffic-light urgency language used for seller deadlines (S-07).

H-05/H-06 — money truth, then insight

  • H-05 settlements table shows the hub's share per checkout and surfaces dispute holds explicitly — a "Dispute hold" row can't be settled until resolved (FR-10.3), never silently paid or hidden.
  • H-06 carries both the "Smart · gets better with data" copy AND a V1.x chip — the AI business dashboard is genuinely post-pilot (Feature Breakdown), and every recommendation still carries its mandatory "Recommended because" reason, same rule as the seller's S-11.
  • Forecast chart is dashed to signal prediction (vs. solid historical bars) — the visual grammar itself distinguishes "what happened" from "what the model guesses," so a hub never mistakes a forecast for a fact.

Handoff notes

  • H-02 writes `products` rows; H-03 approvals gate `inventory_checkouts` creation; H-05 reads `settlements` (Database §3) — the hub side of the exact records the seller creates in S-04–S-10.
  • H-06 recommendations come from the same `/ai/recommendations` service (API §13) as the seller's, scoped to inventory-owner context; `reason_summary` remains a required field.
  • A business can hold both Vendor and Hub roles on one account (additive roles) — the console switcher in the topbar moves between them without re-login.
