Internal, and it looks it

  • Violet accent (not orange/green/blue) plus a shield mark signals "internal Trust & Safety tool" — visually unmistakable from any customer/vendor/hub surface, so a screenshot is instantly recognizable as an internal console, never mistaken for something a user sees.
  • Nav groups by admin intent: Monitor / Resolve / Programs — the console is organized around what a Trust & Safety operator is trying to *do*, not around the data models.

A-01 — triage before detail

  • KPI row leads with marketplace-health vitals (active users, accept rate, GMV, dispute rate), but the "Needs attention now" card is the real point — it routes an operator straight into the specific dispute/fraud/license item that's most urgent, deep-linking into the other screens.
  • Dispute rate is shown as a headline health metric (0.4% / 1k txns) — the success metric named in the Product Vision doc, made operationally visible.

A-02 — the dispute console enforces FR-10.3

  • SLA bars turn red as the 5-business-day target approaches (case #4471 at 88%) — the resolution clock is a first-class visual, since disputes are the one place slow resolution directly erodes trust on both sides.
  • Evidence + a full chain-of-custody timeline sit side by side — condition photos from checkout (S-06) vs. return (S-09) are exactly the artifacts that resolve a consignment dispute fairly, and the AI Vision Verification future feature would slot in here.
  • Four resolution paths (rule for either party / split / request more) — and critically, the timeline explicitly states "Trust Score change held until resolved", making FR-10.3 (never pre-empt a score change) visible in the UI itself.

A-03 — the compliance backbone

  • License review queue is where the `requires_license` gate from vendor onboarding (V-01) actually gets a human decision — approving here is what unblocks a vendor going live.
  • Category suggestions are admin-approved, never self-service (Roadmap Q8) — and the taxonomy table is where `requires_license`/`regulated_by` metadata is actually set, the single source that drives gating everywhere else.

A-04 — flags inform, humans decide

  • An explicit design statement, not just a policy: the console surfaces fraud signals but never auto-bans — the privacy note names the real stakes (a wrongly-banned low-income seller is a genuine harm). Every suspension is an explicit admin action (Security §4).
  • Severity-ranked so high-signal items (device-fingerprint ping-farming, repeated oversell attempts) rise above low-signal noise.

A-06 — impact reporting that doesn't surveil

  • The Shelter Program view is aggregate-only by design (FR-12.3) — counts and totals, never an individual resident's transactions. The privacy note makes that guarantee explicit at the top, because the whole program's dignity depends on not surveilling the people it's meant to help.
  • "Cosigned exposure" column shows each partner's capped liability (Q4) — the shelter's risk is bounded and visible, never open-ended.

A-07 — honest about MVP scope

  • Explicitly labeled manual/record-keeping for the pilot (Roadmap Q9) — the self-serve sponsor dashboard is deferred, and the screen says so rather than pretending a richer product exists.

Handoff notes & milestone

  • A-02 resolution writes to `disputes` (status, resolution, resolved_at) and only then triggers held Trust Score / payout changes (FR-10.2/10.3); A-03 approvals flip `license_documents.status` and `category_suggestions.status`; A-05 suspend sets `users.status`.
  • Least-privilege: support-agent vs. full-admin role tiers (Product Vision doc) mean not every operator sees every action — e.g., payout holds and user suspension may be gated to senior roles.
  • This is screen 53 of 53 designed across the whole app — all 77 catalogued screens' design language is now established (MVP + the V1.x screens shown for continuity). Full spec: docs/13-screen-design-specs.md § A-01–A-07.
